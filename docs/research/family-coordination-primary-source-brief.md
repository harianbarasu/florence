# Family coordination workload: a primary-source product brief

_Research date: August 29, 2026_

## Product conclusion

Florence should not become another family organizer that parents must keep current. It should be the agent that sits across the family's existing messages, inboxes, calendars, documents, apps, and conversations and repeatedly performs one general loop:

1. **Notice** new information or an unresolved objective.
2. **Understand** the people, commitments, dependencies, preferences, and source state.
3. **Reconcile** duplicates, changes, conflicts, and private-versus-household context.
4. **Decide** whether to remember, ask, recommend, act, monitor, or stay quiet.
5. **Do the work** across whatever tools the outcome requires.
6. **Confirm** the external result and update the household picture.
7. **Remember** durable context and reusable artifacts for the next situation.

The recurring domains below are product rehearsals for that same loop, not fixed intents, vertical routers, or separate mini-products.

## Why this matters

- In the U.S. Surgeon General's published 2023 data, 33% of parents reported high stress in the prior month and 48% said their stress was completely overwhelming on most days; named stressors include time demands, finances, children's health and safety, and technology. ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/surgeongeneral/reports-and-publications/parents/index.html))
- The 2025 American Time Use Survey found that 81% of people did household activities on an average day for about two hours. Adults living with children under 13 also averaged 5.1 hours with a child in their care while doing other activities. The survey's definitions span cooking, household management, purchasing services, paperwork, children's health, schoolwork, events, and pickup/drop-off. ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.htm))
- Among different-sex couples in which both parents worked full time, 52% said the mother did more day-to-day parenting and 43% said she did more household chores; mothers and fathers also perceived that division differently. ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2026/06/16/how-working-parents-divide-family-responsibilities/))
- Childcare is not one standard provider relationship: in 2025, 25% of parents with children under 13 used paid care in the prior week, while 44% used unpaid care, often from relatives. ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-living-arrangements-care-work.htm))

**Product inference:** Florence creates value when it removes noticing, translation, follow-through, and handoff work from the parental unit. Merely generating another list or reminding one adult of a fact can preserve the very coordination burden the product is meant to remove.

## The general capabilities underneath those workflows

These are product behaviors, not infrastructure projects:

1. **Universal capture.** Understand messages, email, calendars, links, pages, photos, PDFs, forms, receipts, confirmations, and conversation without requiring the parent to classify the request.
2. **Household reconciliation.** Resolve people and organizations, merge repeated notices, notice changed facts, distinguish personal context from household truth, and keep the live source authoritative.
3. **Durable family memory.** Remember stable context and reusable artifacts indefinitely unless corrected or removed. The 90-day onboarding scan is a discovery window, not a memory expiration policy.
4. **Active-work memory.** Keep each unresolved objective, responsible party, next action, dependency, source evidence, attempts, deadline, and completion condition until an outcome is known.
5. **Compositional action.** Choose and combine whatever tools the outcome requires—search, browser, calendar, messaging, calling, reminders, lists, or external services—without routing through a hardcoded task taxonomy.
6. **Decision compression.** Do the recoverable research and preparation first, then ask one real choice with useful options and household context.
7. **Outcome confirmation.** Close work from external state or an explicit family response. Sending a reminder, submitting a form, or starting a search is progress, not success.
8. **Calibrated proactivity.** Read broadly and retain liberally, but interrupt selectively. A proactive message should be novel, consequential, timely, directed to the right person, and paired with an action or decision.

## A useful Vault shape

The Vault should represent the parental unit's shared operating context, with source-level visibility retained where needed. Sections are views over one connected memory—not separate vertical silos:

- **People and relationships:** adults, children, caregivers, relatives, teachers, coaches, providers, emergency contacts.
- **Schools, care, and activities:** organizations, classes, teams, portals, schedules, policies, pickup rules, recurring obligations.
- **Health and care:** history, allergies, medications, clinicians, insurance, forms, referrals, follow-up.
- **Routines and logistics:** school days, custody or handoff patterns, transport, bedtime, recurring chores, household rhythms.
- **Recipes, meals, and lists:** recipes, preferences, substitutions, successful meals, staples, inventories, shopping and packing lists.
- **Places, providers, and purchases:** trusted services, quotes, sizes, brands, warranties, subscriptions, prior decisions and receipts.
- **Plans and projects:** trips, camps, parties, moves, repairs, applications, ongoing comparisons, reusable checklists.
- **Documents and references:** source links, PDFs, forms, policies, instructions, notes, and artifacts Florence may need later.
- **Preferences and decisions:** family tastes, boundaries, tradeoffs, rejected options, and the reason behind an enduring choice.

Every retained item should preserve its source, affected people, household/private visibility, aliases, last-confirmed time, and relationships to active work. A recipe first seen six months ago, a child's school, or a trusted pediatrician should not vanish because it falls outside a rolling source-search window.

## Proactivity without repetition

Florence should consider speaking when there is a new source change, approaching deadline, schedule conflict, unanswered decision, missing owner, external reply, completed outcome, or materially better option. Before sending, it should answer:

1. Is this still current at the source?
2. Is it new relative to what Florence already told or asked this family?
3. Does someone need to decide or do something now?
4. Is Florence able to do more before interrupting?
5. Who needs to see it: one adult, the parental unit, or the full family thread?
6. What event will resolve it, and when—if ever—would another prompt be useful?

If those questions do not produce a useful next move, Florence should retain the context silently. “All-day family date on September 14” repeated in three phrasings is not proactivity; “The school moved pickup to 2:30, which now overlaps your appointment—want me to ask Sam or move the appointment?” is.

## Product acceptance scenarios

These scenarios test the general loop across different household realities:

1. **School notice:** Florence finds a child-specific deadline in an attachment, reconciles the repeated email and calendar notice, names the responsible adult and next step, and offers to complete the form or payment.
2. **Pickup change:** A childcare message changes pickup; Florence updates the household plan, checks both adults' constraints, asks only the necessary handoff question, and confirms who is doing it.
3. **Busy-week dinner:** Florence uses the actual calendar, saved recipes, dietary preferences, and prior family feedback to propose a practical plan, create the grocery list or cart, and adapt when a night changes.
4. **Youth activity disruption:** A source schedule changes while the subscribed calendar is stale; Florence identifies the live change, checks transport and volunteer obligations, and sends only the revised plan.
5. **Health follow-through:** An appointment produces a referral and medication change; Florence retains the durable record, assigns the follow-up, tracks whether it happened, and does not confuse the reminder with completion.
6. **Family project:** A trip, camp application, party, or home repair evolves over weeks across email, documents, websites, calls, and calendar commitments without losing the objective or flooding the chat.
7. **Dropped thread:** An item discovered during the onboarding scan stays quiet until a later event or request makes it relevant, then resurfaces with its source and an immediately useful next action.
8. **Natural conversation:** Every actionable request receives immediate human-feeling acknowledgement; Florence continues the work, communicates a real blocker plainly, and returns one clear outcome instead of silence or generic refusal.

## What not to build

- another manually maintained family calendar, task board, or feed;
- a fixed router for “school,” “travel,” “shopping,” or other named verticals;
- a digest that mistakes completeness for usefulness;
- a per-parent memory model that cannot form household truth;
- reminders that are treated as proof of completion;
- arbitrary source, memory, or tool-turn caps that silently lower product quality; or
- proactive messages whose only content is an already-known fact.
