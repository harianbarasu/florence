# Instinct X use-case inventory, August 17–31, 2026

## Question

How are people actually using Instinct, especially for work adjacent to parenting and household operations, and what do those reports imply for Florence?

## Method and limits

This inventory combines the existing Florence research ledger with a fresh signed-in X search over August 17–31, 2026. Searches covered general Instinct mentions, first-person formulations such as “using Instinct” and “asked Instinct,” concrete task verbs, quoted posts, and reply threads. The review deliberately retained failures, trust complaints, feature requests, and workarounds instead of collecting only enthusiastic launch posts.

The evidence is a large qualitative sample, not a measured benchmark. X search is not a complete export; some long posts are truncated in search; user accounts are self-reported and are not independent proof of provider-side completion. The safe quantitative description is **dozens of distinct posts and dozens of firsthand users**, substantially broader than the two strategic tweets that prompted this follow-up. Before the fresh pass, Florence's existing notes alone contained 33 unique August 17–31 X status URLs: 23 firsthand tester/user posts, five demand or critique posts, and five founder posts.

## Multiplayer demand and user workarounds

- [Rebecca Kaden](https://x.com/rebeccakaden/status/2094411588950270409) ranks coordination with a husband or friends' agents as her top missing consumer-agent capability and outbound phone calls second.

- [Aaron Frank](https://x.com/arfrank/status/2094097956391784884) asks how to connect his Instinct with his spouse's. His thread exposes two simultaneous requirements: genuinely shared context and private context for matters such as surprises.
- [Ana Wiechers](https://x.com/AnaWiechers/status/2094202915254935577) says two spouses' agents communicated through their WhatsApp accounts with some success, but the workaround was pulled into the human accounts and lacked a clean agent identity.
- [Nathaniel McNamara](https://x.com/NathanielMc/status/2094165276552290750) reports an Instinct-proposed shared Google Sheet as a makeshift shared memory. This is a user's account of the suggestion, not an official multiplayer capability.
- [Sawit](https://x.com/tansawit/status/2094442071671398691) explicitly names family use as a missing capability despite otherwise strong multi-week use.

This is stronger evidence than a single wish-list tweet: multiple users are independently asking for spouse/family coordination and trying improvised bridges. Belsky's framing clarifies the actual consumer outcome: reduce the remembering, reminding, checking, and resentment-producing follow-up that currently passes between partners. It also shows why “put both adults in one chat” is insufficient. A household agent needs shared work, private evidence, explicit audience boundaries, independent authority, and a way to pursue loose ends without turning Florence herself into a new source of nagging.

## Implications for Florence

The corrected conclusion is not that Instinct owns solo mode while Florence owns parent errands. Instinct users already report many parent and family-adjacent errands: school forms, lunch menus, family travel, child seating, groceries, medical communication, subscriptions, insurance, portals, gifts, and reservations.

Florence therefore needs both layers:

1. **Match the individual execution baseline.** An ordinary text must become persistent work across email, calendars, public pages, documents, browsers, payments, and eventually calls, with honest progress and verified closure.
2. **Turn individual completion into a household outcome.** Reconcile sources from either adult, protect private evidence, ask the right adult one focused question, respect guardian and spending authority, update dependent plans, and close the obligation once in the family group.

The clean product thesis is:

> Instinct can complete an errand involving a child for one user. Florence should coordinate the obligation across the adults responsible for that child, preserve each person's privacy and authority, update the rest of family life, and close it once for the household.

The strongest demonstrations are consequently not generic task lists. They are:

- a school notice received by either adult, correctly resolved to the child, completed with one focused question, calendared, and closed with a receipt;
- a family-travel disruption reconciled across every traveler and both adults, with seats, transport, artifacts, and shared plans repaired;
- a pediatric appointment completed across private health context, two calendars, travel time, a real phone call when required, provider confirmation, and household follow-up.
